## Retrieval failure vs generation failure

Both retrieval arms query the same index, so their retrieval failures are
the same items. That makes the decomposition clean:

| | A2_base_rag | A4_ft_rag |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval failure (source never retrieved) | 18 | 18 |
| Generation failure (source retrieved, still wrong) | 31 | 17 |

Retrieval failures are **identical (18 items)**, as they must be.
So the entire accuracy difference between the two retrieval arms comes
from generation: 31 → 17 errors. That is the adapter's real
contribution, measured with the retriever held fixed.

It also fixes a ceiling. Neither arm can exceed the retriever's hit rate,
so ~18 items are unreachable without a better index — no amount of
fine-tuning addresses them.

**Example — right act wrong section**

> **Q.** In what circumstances may the Judge permit evidence of one alleged fact to be given before another alleged fact is proved?
> **Gold.** The relevancy of one alleged fact depends upon another alleged fact being first proved. — `bsa2023:141`
> **Cited.** `The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, §54`

```
**Answer.** A judge may permit evidence of one alleged fact to be given before another alleged fact is proved if it is necessary to make the proof of the first fact effective.

**Why.** This provision allows for the admissibility of evidence in a manner that ensures the integrity and coherence of the trial process, ensuring that relevant evidence can be presented in a logical sequence when it is necessary for the understanding and evaluation of the facts.

**Source.** The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, §54

**Example — generation failure**

> **Q.** What is the implication when one party in a contract fails to provide something that is necessary for the other party to perform their promise?
> **Gold.** The non-performing party is bound to make compensation to the other party for any loss caused by the non-performance. — `contract1872:54`
> **Cited.** `The Indian Contract Act, 1872, §53`

```
**Answer.** The contract becomes voidable at the option of the party whose performance is dependent on the other party's promise. This party can choose to terminate the contract and seek compensation for any loss sustained due to the non-performance.

**Why.** When one party's obligation is contingent on the other party fulfilling their promise, the failure of the latter to do so means the former cannot be compelled to fulfill their part of the agreement. Therefore, the contract can be terminated by the affected party, who can also claim compensation for any losses incurred as a result of the non-performance.

**Source.** The Indian Contract Act, 1872, §53
```

**Example — right act wrong section**

> **Q.** What is the condition under which evidence of a fact must be proved before evidence of another fact is given?
> **Gold.** The fact proposed to be proved is one of which evidence is admissible only upon proof of some other fact. — `bsa2023:141`
> **Cited.** `The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, §145`

```
**Answer.** Evidence of a fact cannot be given unless the fact itself has been proved.

**Why.** The law mandates that evidence of any fact cannot be given until that fact has first been proved. This rule applies to all facts, regardless of their nature or importance.

**Source.** The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, §145
