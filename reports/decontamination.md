# Decontamination

Generated by `ragft.dataset.decontaminate`. **Do not hand-edit.**

The guarantee comes from ordering, not from these checks: sections are assigned
to splits **before** any QA is generated, so a training pair and an eval
question cannot share a source passage. These four checks *verify* that
guarantee rather than trying to establish it after the fact.

## Enforcement

Val questions removed as semantic duplicates of train questions: **2**
(324 -> 322), at the pre-registered cosine threshold
0.95.

Val exists to monitor overfitting, so a val question that paraphrases a training
question would make val loss understate the generalisation gap -- the one
measurement val is there to provide.

These were genuine duplicates, not checker artefacts. Textbooks define the same
concept in several places, so two genuinely different sections produce the same
question: *Biology* 31.1 and *Anatomy and Physiology* 2.4 both define "organic
compound". Structural provenance cannot catch that, which is precisely why the
embedding check exists.

**9 pairs sit above a stricter 0.90 and were left in
place.** Tightening a threshold after seeing the numbers is exactly the post-hoc
adjustment this project exists to avoid, so the residual is reported instead of
acted on.

## A false positive worth recording

The first run flagged 420 within-train pairs. Inspection showed the top matches
were "What is phagocytosis?" against "What is chemical energy?" -- unrelated
three-token questions. Too short to form any 5-gram shingle, they produced
*empty* MinHash signatures, and empty signatures are identical to one another,
so every short question matched every other one.

That is a bug in the checker, not contamination in the data -- and it is the
more dangerous direction of error, because a check that cries wolf gets its
threshold relaxed until it stops catching real problems. Shingling now degrades
to unigrams below the shingle length, and `tests/test_decontamination.py` pins
the behaviour so it cannot regress.
